Case Study · Maaketing

The founder wasn’t camera-ready.
Eight months later, one video hit 100,000 views.

Client: Bhagini Fashions — women’s ethnic wear (kurtis, kurta sets, plazo sets), Nasik. Working together since September 2024.
1,200+ followers in year one 100K views, one video 90% content approved first pass

01 The brief she wanted, the call we made

She came to us wanting a model. Her store sells to women who buy ethnic wear for themselves — mostly local, mostly middle-aged, mostly shopping for how the outfit fits their own body. A young hired face wasn’t going to make that customer feel seen. We said so on day one.

What she asked for

A professional model, polished shoots — the standard local playbook.

What we proposed instead

The founder herself on camera — because she is the customer.

02 We let the data argue, not us

She wasn’t convinced yet, so we tested her instinct properly — two different models, a real chance each. Neither built a connection with the audience. The results made the case we couldn’t.

03 Four months in, she stepped in front of the camera

The first videos were rough — new to camera, limited stock that fit her. We kept the strategy steady. Slowly the content became what it actually was: a customer walking into the store and being shown each piece personally, one outfit at a time — the in-store experience, filmed.

04 The discovery: one word on screen changed performance

We found that videos naming Nasik — not just spoken, but kept as simple on-screen text for the whole video — consistently outperformed. Obvious in hindsight, easy to miss in practice: every viewer meets the page for the first time on whatever video they land on. Naming the city says we’re local, we’re near you, you can walk in — instantly.

No prices shown on screen, ever — that’s what turned views into DMs and walk-ins asking to see the piece in person.

05 Raw on purpose, while everyone else polished

We deliberately kept the content unpolished — simple, real, shot the way it happens. In a feed full of slick studio edits, raw reads as honest. It made people believe: this is a real shop, this is really her.

What competitors were doing

Polished, produced, studio-lit — and forgettable.

What we did

Handheld, honest, store-floor real — and believable.

06 June: eight months of groundwork landed at once

A lighter, funnier video — a second content pillar we’d built deliberately to break up the constant product pitch — crossed 100,000 views. It wasn’t an isolated hit: it pulled attention back onto the product videos too, driving real price inquiries and real footfall into the store.

07 Priced to keep her growing, not to hit a number

When her margins got tight, we lowered our fee more than once — not because we had slack, but because we were building something with her, not billing her. She’s back to asking for the full plan this month, on her own.

“Other pages keep messaging her cheaper offers. She screenshots them for us — and laughs it off.” — on the working relationship, present day
