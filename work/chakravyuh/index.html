When every gaming cafe in Nasik
raised its prices, only one stayed fully booked.

Client: VRP’s Chakravyuh — Ashoka Marg, Nasik. Esports gaming cafe: PS5 lounge (3 private rooms), 20 PC builds, 2 racing simulators, in-house cafe. Working together since February 2025.

// LOG_01Inherited Setup

Inherited a broken machine

The previous agency charged ₹12,000 a month for testimonials that asked gamers which games they played — never the experience, never the venue. Of a ₹1,000 monthly ad budget, roughly ₹100 actually reached Meta. The founder didn’t know enough to catch it. He just knew nothing was working.

// LOG_02Audience Read

Who's actually paying to be here

Not "gamers" as a category — specifically 12 to 30-year-olds near a school up the road, most without a PS5 or a real gaming PC at home. They weren't coming to network or build a resume. They were coming to escape whatever the day had been, good or bad. That reframe decided the content: funny, relatable, benefit-of-gaming posts — not venue promotion.

// LOG_03Rebrand

One brand, two venues, one confused customer

His original venue and this one had nearly identical names — and very different prices for the same PS5 slot. Customers were recharging at the cheaper Ashoka Marg account, then trying to redeem it at the pricier venue. Neither account named its location anywhere. We rebuilt the handle and bio around his personal brand plus an explicit "Ashoka Marg," everywhere it could appear.

// LOG_05Growth Signal

Followers were never the point

50 to 322 — modest, and that's fine. The real signal was behavioral: players started rewatching their own gameplay clips on the page, which built recall, which meant longer sessions and more repeat visits. Growth as a proxy for something happening in the room, not a number on its own.

// LOG_06Pricing Engine

Engineering the booking board

The client wanted a straightforward price hike ahead of an early-starting peak season. Instead of just raising rates, we restructured the incentive: the old discount stayed — but only for customers who committed to a 2-hour booking upfront.

Every 2-hour, multi-controller booking lands at exactly ₹100 per controller, per hour — the pre-hike discount, preserved, but now it has to be booked ahead instead of walked in on.

That single change solved two problems at once: customers felt like they were getting a deal, and the cafe could finally tell a waiting walk-in exactly when a seat would open — "free by 7, I'll hold it for you" — instead of an open-ended wait that used to sour the experience either way.

// LOG_07Market Proof

The price hike that emptied every other cafe in Nasik

When the hike went live, his own original venue at College Road raised prices by a similar margin — and was visibly empty within a week. So were the other gaming cafes around it. Ashoka Marg didn't blink: 8 to 10-hour bookings, fully booked, straight through peak season.
